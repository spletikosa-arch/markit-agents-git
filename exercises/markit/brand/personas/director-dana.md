# Persona: Director Dana

## Demographics & Role
- **Age:** 40-50
- **Title:** Marketing Director
- **Company:** Mid-size company, 50-500 employees
- **Team:** Manages a marketing team of 5-15 across content, demand gen, and design — often distributed or hybrid

## Pain Points & Frustrations
- Her team's creative and strategic work (campaign planning, content development) keeps getting fragmented by meetings — the deep work suffers even though the org values it.
- She's measured on output and campaign velocity, but her team's calendars are so full of syncs that "shipping" keeps sliding.
- Cross-functional coordination with sales, product, and exec stakeholders means her team's time isn't fully hers to protect.
- She's evaluated productivity tools before that promised a lot and delivered a dashboard nobody used after month one.

## Goals & Motivations
- Increase campaign throughput without asking for more headcount.
- Be able to show leadership, in concrete terms, that protecting focus time improved output — she needs metrics, not vibes.
- Reduce meeting load across her team in a way that doesn't require her to be the "no meetings" enforcer in every calendar invite dispute.
- Free up her own time from calendar Tetris so she can spend more of it on strategy.

## Current Tools
- Google Workspace, Slack, Zoom, Asana/Monday for campaign tracking
- HubSpot or similar for marketing ops
- Has used RescueTime in the past for individual time tracking but found it didn't solve team-level coordination

## How Planerio Helps Her
- Coordinates focus time at the team level, not just the individual level — which is the gap she's felt with tools like RescueTime.
- Gives her a defensible story for leadership: more protected focus time, tied to campaign velocity, without a heavy-handed policy she has to enforce personally.
- Fits into the stack she already runs (Slack, Zoom, Google Workspace), so it's a fast pilot, not a quarter-long rollout.
- Positions her as ahead of the curve on AI-powered team ops — a plus for her own visibility with leadership.

## Information Sources She Trusts
- Marketing ops communities and newsletters (e.g., MarketingOps.com, Superpath)
- Peer directors at similar-sized companies — she compares notes at conferences and in Slack communities
- Analyst or industry reports that quantify productivity/focus-time impact
- Vendor case studies, but she wants the metrics, not just the testimonial quote
