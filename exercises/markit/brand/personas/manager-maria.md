# Persona: Manager Maria

## Demographics & Role
- **Age:** 35-45
- **Title:** Engineering Manager / Distributed Team Manager
- **Team:** Manages 10-15 developers spread across 3-4 time zones
- **Company:** Tech company, 50-500 employees
- **Reports to:** VP of Engineering or Director of Engineering

## Pain Points & Frustrations
- Her calendar is a wall of back-to-back 1:1s and status meetings — she rarely gets uninterrupted time herself, and neither does her team.
- Coordinating a single deep-work block across time zones means playing calendar Tetris manually, every week.
- She can see individual calendars but has no real visibility into whether the team collectively has enough protected focus time.
- Async handoffs slip because someone's "focus time" overlaps with someone else's only overlap window with a different region.
- She's wary of tools that feel like surveillance — she wants coordination, not a productivity scorecard she has to justify to her boss.

## Goals & Motivations
- Protect her team's deep-work time without becoming the person who has to police it.
- Ship on schedule despite the time-zone tax on coordination.
- Be seen by leadership as running an efficient, low-friction team — without burning out doing it herself.
- Reduce the number of meetings that exist just to compensate for poor scheduling.

## Current Tools
- Google Calendar (primary), Slack, Zoom, Jira/Linear for sprint tracking
- Has tried manually blocking "no meeting Wednesdays" — inconsistent adoption
- Looked at Clockwise but found it individual-focused rather than team-coordinated

## How Planerio Helps Her
- Automatically finds and blocks team-wide focus time across time zones, so she's not manually reconciling calendars.
- Gives her visibility into team focus-time health without turning it into individual surveillance — coordination, not control.
- Integrates directly with the Slack/Zoom/Google Workspace stack she already runs, so there's no migration project to sell to her team.
- Cuts the "let's find a time that works" thread count to near zero.

## Information Sources She Trusts
- Peer recommendations from other eng managers (Slack communities, EM-focused newsletters like Engineering Manager Weekly)
- Case studies with real before/after metrics, not vendor claims
- Product Hunt and G2 reviews from people in similar roles
- Short demo videos over long sales calls — she'll evaluate a tool herself before looping in anyone else
